With a vauge feeling of relief, he set the instruments of the failed project down on the painted wooden table beside him. The project was one of many, an attempt to capture the hidden sounds of the world usually imperceptible to human hearing.  But the piezoelectric microphone that he had planned to connect to his audio recorder had been damaged, shattered during the voyage or crushed by other items in the cardboard box where it had been stored for many years waiting to be utilized.  He had packed the tiny microphone and several other devices in a hurry, trying to second guess his own scattered mind as he prepared for the journey to his childhood home.  His mind had been vibrating on a city frequency, the buzzing of canibis and alcohol in his skull blending with the hum of traffic and electric lights.  But now in the cabin, hundreds of miles from the nearest town, his mind was somewhat clearer and he didn't feel the pressing need to accomplish things. The desperate and frantic longing for some sense of fulfillment through action or through the execution of some grand experiment that would justify his existence had somewhat faded over the last few days, and the contented calm of the forest's slow moving timeline had began to slow his thoughts towards the speed of moss growing. He sat in the old armchair with its distinct floral pattern from the 1970s and wondered how many of these projects would bear the fruit of some kind of lasting importance in the chronicals of his personal story.  The idea of discovering a new sound never before heard by human ears and somehow capturing and presenting this sound to an audience had always been a source of inspiration to him, but today all that seemed a little less important. Instead that sounds of the distant lake waves breaking on the shore and the faint swishing of trees in the wind seemed much more real, sounds older than human ears in a place evolving at the speed of water wearing down a river bed year after year.  Of course nothing was stagnant, the silence would be broken by the snap of an old tree finally letting go of it's hold on the earth or by the rush of mud and rocks rushing down in a landslide, but these punctuations were rare and it would be easy to believe that this place had essentially remained the same since long before his arrival.
